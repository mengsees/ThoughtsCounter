Thoughts Counter during meditation

Tap each time you notice a thought. When you finish, see how dense your thoughts were and how far apart they came.

min

How it works

Settle in

You get 10 seconds after pressing Start. The clock begins once you are ready.

On a phone

Tap the big button, or anywhere on the screen, for each thought. Tap Undo if you slip.

On a computer

Press Space or left-click anywhere. Backspace or a right-click undoes the last one.

Finish

It ends by itself at the set length. Or tap End (twice), or press Esc twice.

What you will see

Timeline

When each thought came, with your longest quiet stretch shaded.

Density

Thoughts per minute, with a smoothed trend line.

Example data. You also get a chart of the gaps between thoughts.

Why it helps

Why count your thoughts?

  • Noticing is the practice. Each tap marks the moment you realise your attention has drifted from your anchor. That moment of recognition is the skill you are training.
  • It makes the mind visible. Thoughts feel constant and shapeless. Counting turns them into separate events you can see, compare and come back to.
  • It keeps you out of the story. A quick tap is lighter than following a thought or judging yourself for having it.
  • It gives you something to look at afterwards. Patterns that are hard to feel in the moment show up clearly on the chart.

What you can observe

  • Settling time. How long did it take for the first thought to come?
  • Quiet stretches. How long was your longest gap, and what came right before it?
  • Bursts or an even flow. Thoughts often arrive in clusters. A cluster can follow a trigger such as a sound, an itch or a worry.
  • First half vs. second half. Do you settle as the session goes on, or get restless?
  • Across sessions. Use the notes (sleep, time of day, stress, caffeine) and the history chart to see what changes the pattern.

A higher count is not worse. As you get better at noticing, you may catch more thoughts, so look at the gaps and the trend rather than treating the number as a score. This is a self-report tool for curiosity, not a medical measurement.
